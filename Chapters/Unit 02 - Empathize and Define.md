## 1. Why Empathy Comes First

> "If I had an hour to solve a problem, I'd spend 55 minutes thinking about the problem and 5 minutes thinking about solutions." — attributed to Albert Einstein

Most failed products solve the **wrong problem**. Empathy helps you:

- understand what users **actually** need, not what you assume;
- discover **hidden needs** that users cannot easily put into words;
- see the gap between what people **say** and what they **do**.

### Empathy vs Sympathy

| Sympathy | Empathy |
|---|---|
| "I feel sorry for you." | "I understand how you feel." |
| Looks at the person from outside | Steps into the person's shoes |
| Can create distance | Creates connection and insight |

## 2. Empathy Research Methods

| Method | What it is | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **User interviews** | One-to-one conversations with open-ended questions | To understand motivations and feelings |
| **Observation** | Watching users do a task without interrupting | To see real behaviour |
| **Contextual inquiry** | Observing *and* asking questions in the user's real setting | To understand work in context |
| **Immersion** | Experiencing the situation yourself | To feel the pain points personally |
| **Surveys** | Short questionnaires to many people | To check how common a finding is |
| **Extreme users** | Talking to people at the edges (power users, non-users) | To reveal needs that ordinary users hide |

### Tips for good interviews

- Ask **open** questions: "Tell me about the last time you…"
- Ask **"Why?"** repeatedly (the *5 Whys*).
- Ask for **stories**, not opinions.
- Look for **contradictions** between words and actions.
- **Don't** suggest answers or pitch your idea.
- Record exact quotes. They are powerful evidence later.

> ❌ "Would you use an app that reminds you about assignments?"
> ✅ "Tell me about the last time you missed a deadline. What happened?"

## 3. Contextual Inquiry

**Contextual inquiry** is a research technique where the designer observes and interviews users **in their real environment**: at the workplace, at home, at the point of use. It is not done in a lab or through a survey.

It follows a **master–apprentice** model: the user is the expert; you are the learner.

### Four principles

1. **Context**: go to where the work happens.
2. **Partnership**: the user and researcher explore the work together.
3. **Interpretation**: share your interpretations with the user and check them.
4. **Focus**: keep a clear research question in mind.

**Example:** To improve the college fee-payment process, sit with students at the accounts counter during the fee deadline week, watch what happens and ask questions as they wait.

## 4. Empathy Maps

An **empathy map** is a simple visual tool that captures what you know about one user or user type.

```
 ┌────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┐
 │         SAYS           │        THINKS          │
 │  Direct quotes         │  What occupies their   │
 │                        │  mind (may not say it) │
 ├──────────────── USER ──┼────────────────────────┤
 │         DOES           │        FEELS           │
 │  Actions & behaviour   │  Emotions, worries,    │
 │  you observed          │  excitement            │
 └────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┘
          PAINS (frustrations)  |  GAINS (wants & needs)
```

### Example: A first-year student using a crypto wallet for the first time

| Quadrant | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Says** | "I don't understand what a seed phrase is." · "What if I send money to the wrong address?" |
| **Thinks** | "Everyone else seems to know this." · "I might lose all my money." |
| **Does** | Watches YouTube tutorials · Screenshots the seed phrase · Sends very small test amounts |
| **Feels** | Anxious, confused, curious, embarrassed to ask |
| **Pains** | Technical jargon, fear of irreversible mistakes |
| **Gains** | Confidence, a simple step-by-step guide, an undo or safety check |

> 💡 The most valuable insights often sit in the gap between **Says** and **Does**.

## 5. Personas

A **persona** is a realistic, research-based character that represents a group of users.

**Example persona:**

> **Name:** Riya, 18 · First-year BCA student, Bengaluru
> **Goal:** Submit assignments on time while managing hostel life and a part-time job.
> **Frustrations:** Deadlines are spread across WhatsApp groups, email and the LMS.
> **Quote:** "I don't forget the work. I forget *where* it was announced."
> **Tech comfort:** High on mobile, low on desktop portals.

Personas keep the team focused on a **real person** instead of an "average user".

## 6. User Journey Maps

A **journey map** shows the steps a user goes through to achieve a goal, with their feelings at each step.

| Stage | Hear about deadline | Find the brief | Do the work | Submit | Get feedback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Actions** | Sees WhatsApp message | Searches LMS | Works late at night | Uploads PDF | Waits for marks |
| **Feelings** | 😐 | 😟 Confused | 😩 Stressed | 😰 Upload fails | 😐 Unclear feedback |
| **Opportunities** | One notification channel | Clear brief location | Early reminders | Upload confirmation | Rubric-based feedback |

The **low points** in the emotion line are your best design opportunities.

## 7. The Define Stage: From Insights to Problem Statements

After research, you have lots of notes. The **Define** stage turns them into a clear problem worth solving.

### Step 1: Synthesise with affinity mapping

1. Write each observation on a sticky note.
2. Group similar notes into clusters.
3. Name each cluster with an **insight** (a surprising truth about users).

### Step 2: Write a Point-of-View (POV) statement

> **[User]** needs **[need]** because **[insight]**.

**Example:**
> *A first-year student juggling a part-time job* **needs** *a single place to see all deadlines* **because** *announcements are scattered across three channels and they forget where each one was posted.*

### Good vs bad problem statements

| ❌ Bad | Why | ✅ Better |
|---|---|---|
| "Build an assignment app." | It's a solution, not a problem | "Students need one trusted place to see deadlines because…" |
| "Improve student life." | Too broad | "Hostel students need quiet study space after 10 PM because…" |
| "Students are lazy." | Blames the user | "Students need early reminders because they underestimate task length." |

### Step 3: Turn the POV into "How Might We" (HMW) questions

- **How** → assumes a solution exists.
- **Might** → it's okay if it doesn't work.
- **We** → it's a team effort.

**Examples:**
- HMW make every deadline visible in one place?
- HMW help students estimate how long a task will take?
- HMW make submitting an assignment feel effortless?

> 💡 A good HMW is **not too broad** ("HMW fix education?") and **not too narrow** ("HMW add a red button to the LMS?").

## 8. IBM Connection: Hills

IBM's **Hills** are like problem statements written as goals:

> **Who:** A first-year BCA student
> **What:** can see all assignment deadlines from every course in one view
> **Wow:** within 10 seconds of opening their phone, with zero missed deadlines in a semester.

3. Create **one empathy map** and **one persona**.

5. Write **one POV statement** and **three HMW questions**.
